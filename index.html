How these numbers were measured

Read-only snapshot of the open Vung Tau project on 24 November 2026. Source duration is the sum of unique primary video files in its source bins: file frames ÷ source frame rate, deduplicated by file path. Proxy files, thumbnails, separate audio, stock and repeated timeline placements are excluded from that total. It measures recorded video duration, not editing hours or elapsed shooting time.

The four review timelines also contain repeated placements and stock, so their combined lengths are not used as the raw-footage total. The final timeline spans 118.8355 seconds at 59.94 fps. Its main picture track has 52 segments referencing 36 distinct video sources, including four additional sources outside the primary camera inventory. Its five upper video tracks contain 145 translation, title and caption clips; eight audio tracks contain 84 placements. These are timeline items, not counts of unique sound files or revisions.

The diagrams use measured durations and clip positions. Source rows concatenate unique files by camera folder; they are an inventory view, not a reconstruction of shooting order. Picture and finishing views use actual timeline positions. Only anonymous source identifiers, camera categories and timing metadata are shown. No raw footage, frames, dialogue, filenames or local file paths are published. This snapshot does not verify whether a later exported film differs from the current timeline.
